# Design system

Expressive Gmail is Gmail as Google would ship it in Material 3 Expressive, translated to a desktop browser and pushed past the Android app. Every value comes from Google's own tokens (androidx compose material3, spec v0_14_0) unless noted.

## Color

- Roles are `--md-sys-color-*`, set at runtime by `src/app/theme/ThemeProvider.tsx`.
- **Google** (default): the hand-tuned web palette Gmail, Drive and Gemini use (`theme/google-palette.ts`). Neutral surfaces, blue accents.
- **Material You**: any seed through material-color-utilities, 2025 spec, Tonal spot / Vibrant / Expressive / Neutral, three contrast levels. Each style sets its own chroma, so only a seed's hue tells two apart: the 14 presets sit about 26 degrees apart round the wheel. Random rolls a new seed on each click, at least 45 degrees from the last so every roll looks different, and remembers it (`randomSeed`) to show it as chosen. While it's the theme, every page load rolls again, to a seed rolled ahead (`nextRandomSeed`) so theme-boot.js can paint it before the app loads, and each open tab keeps the colour it rolled. Your own colour comes from the system colour picker, follows the drag every 80ms, and is kept (`customSeed`) while a preset is in use, so its swatch brings it back.
- Light, dark and system. Semantic app tokens in `app.css`: `--app-frame` (surface-container), `--row-unread` (lowest), `--row-read` (low), `--row-selected` (secondary-container), `--app-pane`, `--app-menu-bg`.
- **Your picture** (`features/wallpaper/`): At a glance's mark shows your profile photo, or a picture you drop on it or pick from its menu (see Reading pane). It is managed only there; Settings has no picture section. A picture never recolors the app by itself. While one is shown, its colours are one more theme choice: "Use colors from this picture" in its menu and the command palette, a "From your picture" swatch in Quick settings, and "From your picture" beside Google and Material You under Colors in Settings > Appearance. Choosing it again steps to the picture's next colour. With no picture, none of these appear. The frame and every surface keep their usual roles, so nothing about legibility changes.
- **Seeds from a picture**: QuantizerCelebi (128 colours) on about 112 x 112 pixels, then Score for eight, in a worker. Score's k-means starts from `Math.random` in the TypeScript port, so it runs under a fixed LCG (Android's seed, 0x42688) and a picture always gives the same theme. Photos are mostly skin, sky, sand and shadow, so Score often ranks a near-grey or a shadow first: colourful (HCT chroma 16 and up), lit (tone 20 to 92) candidates move ahead, tinted greys under chroma 8 drop, and four remain. Nothing usable (grayscale, nearly transparent) means Google's palette. A profile photo is analysed the same way, from its own bytes, the first time its colours are offered. Using a picture's colours applies its first and keeps style, contrast and light/dark; the theme change cross-fades as usual.
- **Uploads**: decoded through an `<img>` (EXIF orientation, CMYK, an animated GIF or WebP's first frame, SVG as inert pixels), a cut-off JPEG or PNG refused (a phone's motion photo, with its clip after the JPEG, is fine), resized in halving or doubling steps to 2560 on the long edge, stored as WebP 0.8 (JPEG 0.82 where the browser can't encode WebP, PNG with transparency) in IndexedDB (`bg-wallpaper`, one slot), global rather than per account. A saliency map and subject box (a compact skin patch that meets the salient region, or the salient region; faces from the Shape Detection API where available) are kept for cropping.

## Shape

Scale 0/4/8/12/16/20/28/32/48/full. Segmented lists (mail rows, settings rows): 2px gaps, 16px outer and 4px inner corners; hover rounds to 12, selection to 16. Panes and cards 28; the reading pane keeps its surface when empty. Snackbars 28px (a step up from SnackbarTokens' 4dp so the 40px close button 8px in sits concentric), 56 tall, inverse surface, above every other layer. Offline is a standing snackbar at the bottom start; snackbars stack above it. Buttons morph squarer on press (S: 20 → 8). No borders on one side of anything.

## Type

Google Sans Flex (OFL, bundled, latin + latin-ext, wght 300-800 with opsz and ROND). M3 baseline scale as `.md-*` classes, emphasized variants with `.md-emphasized`. Large titles (list hero, thread subject, summary cards) use `ROND 100`. Icons: Material Symbols Rounded subset (`npm run fonts`), FILL animates on selection.

## Motion

Springs as CSS `linear()` curves: effects (damping 1.0) and spatial (damping 0.8), as `--md-sys-motion-{spatial,effects}-{fast,default,slow}`. Google's fast spatial spring (0.6) overshoots about 9%; we use the 0.8 curve for UI so nothing bounces. Spatial = position, size, shape; effects = color and opacity. Exits run on effects and are shorter than entrances. All respect `prefers-reduced-motion` (spatial motion off, fades kept short).

- **Rail**: only the rail's menu button changes it (no hover peek; Compose and destinations never expand it). The grid column switches in one step so the list and reading pane are never laid out per frame: `features/shell/Morph.tsx` moves every `data-morph` element on DefaultSpatial with transforms (FastSpatial for the modal rail). Icons move from top to start; each label cross-fades, a copy of the old one fading out as it rides with its icon while the new one fades in where it lands and drifts the last quarter of the way (never scaled), the active indicator grows from 56x32 to the content pill, the brand travels between the rail and the top bar, and the search bar does not jump. The main area is laid out once at its new width and slides from its old position with its trailing edge held (a frame-colour cover with inverse corners when it widens, an underlay in the pane surface when it narrows); views without a trailing surface move their leading edge with layout. The rail is clipped at the content's leading edge below the header row. Counts and badges cross-fade, the collapsed badge a beat late so it never lands on a fading label; Splits and Labels fade out in place and, expanding, fade in once the old labels are mostly gone. Only the rail's own elements morph: the main area moves as one piece, so a thread's photos and names stay in their cards. The menu glyph swaps menu and menu_open with a 45 degree turn and a 0.6 scale on FastSpatial. More opens on DefaultSpatial with its items fading and rising 8px in a 20ms stagger and closes on FastSpatial; More and Less cross-fade with a short roll. The modal rail unfolds from the collapsed edge with a clip; closing, it folds back on DefaultEffects while fading out in 150ms, so the docked rail underneath takes over instead of swapping in on the last frame.
- **Buttons**: press shape on DefaultEffects (no overshoot); chips, toggle groups and menu shapes on FastSpatial. Extended FABs grow their label track 0fr to 1fr (FastSpatial out, DefaultSpatial in). The filter-chip check grows in from the start.
- **Surfaces**: menus, selects and context menus unroll from the edge next to their anchor: a clip reveal on FastSpatial with a 4px drift and a FastEffects fade, the width fixed from the first frame; they leave on a FastEffects fade. A select in a settings row lines up with its trailing edge. Every modal (dialogs, settings, the date picker, a dialog opened from another) sits on the scrim role at 40% in light and 64% in dark (quick settings and the palette at 24% and 48%), since M3's 32% all but vanishes over a dark theme, fading on DefaultEffects; tooltips scale on FastSpatial; dialogs, settings and full-screen compose on DefaultSpatial; the quick settings sheet slides in on DefaultSpatial. The search panel unrolls with a clip; the selection toolbar grows out of its leading button. Compose motion is described under Compose.
- **Threads**: opening or closing a message animates its card's height on DefaultSpatial (measured before and after the swap, Web Animations) so nothing below it jumps, and the new contents fade in over 230ms with a 4px drift (150ms when closing); messages unfolded from "N older messages" drop 8px into place in a 25ms stagger. Settings sections settle in (250ms, 8px rise, emphasized decelerate) when you switch. Inside a section, rows come in titled runs (Sending, Reading, Privacy...) under Android's category headers: LabelLarge 600 in primary on the rows' 16px text line; search drops the run titles and keeps each run's corners. The sidebar ends with Switch to classic Gmail (a phone puts it in the top app bar). Labels only appears when the connection can edit labels. Settings > Sidebar arranges the rail (`features/shell/sidebarLayout.ts`, stored as `sidebar`): every destination is Always shown, Under More or Hidden, in one list with those headings between the runs, dragged by a handle (or moved with the arrow keys, stepping across a heading into the next place) while the other rows slide on FastSpatial; Splits and Labels can be reordered and switched off. Inbox can move but never leaves the rail. Scheduled left under More still comes up beside Sent while mail waits to send. More disappears when nothing is under it. Right-clicking a rail destination offers Move to More or Always show, Hide from sidebar (with Undo) and Customize sidebar.
- **At a glance**: the mark turns in from 0.5 on the spring and its rows stagger 30ms, once per session; coming back from a conversation it only fades (FastEffects).
- **Arrow keys** (`features/shortcuts/useShortcuts.ts`, `focus.ts`) are off by default: Settings > General > Arrow key navigation (`arrowKeys`). On, they and Enter and Esc work with letter shortcuts off, since they type nothing, and the `?` dialog lists them. In the list, Up and Down move the cursor row; Up from the first conversation reaches the categories (the category rows beside a reading pane, else the open tab, where Left and Right move along the tabs and Enter opens one) and Down comes back. Enter, from a row or the page, opens the conversation and hands focus to its scroll area, so Up, Down, Space and Home/End scroll it the browser's own way; from anywhere else in the conversation (the toolbar, an email's frame) the first arrow press scrolls and moves focus there. Esc goes back to the row. In the sidebar Up and Down step through destinations and Right goes to the list. The scroll area draws no ring; tabs take an inset one, since the scrolling tab row clips outlines.
- **Content**: rows stagger in by 20ms (done within 400ms) and collapse when they leave; threads and pages settle in 200ms because j / k runs all day. The command palette has no motion: it is keyboard-first.
- **Feedback** (`ui/feedback.tsx`): a copy answers in place instead of with a snackbar. The glyph turns into a check (FastSpatial, 45 degree turn, from 0.4 scale), the label rolls up to "Copied" while the button's width follows on FastSpatial, the container takes tertiary (tertiary container for tonal, text, icon buttons and chips) and squares off like a selected toggle, and after 2 seconds it all settles back. Screen readers hear it through a polite live region. Buttons take `confirmed`, chips use `CopyChip`; failures still get a snackbar.
- **Toggles**: selecting an icon toggle pops its glyph from 0.5 on FastSpatial while it fills; the star also turns one point (72 degrees) so it lands on the same shape. Deselecting dips to 0.8. Only real changes animate, never a first render or a switch to another thread.
- **Selection and state**: checkbox checks wipe in from the start as the box fills, and the box squeezes to 0.9 while pressed; a selected row's check draws in 60ms after its disc turns. Shift-click (and Shift X) selects Gmail style from the anchor, the last row checked or unchecked by hand: the range takes the anchor's state, or flips when that would change nothing. While Shift is held over a row, the rows the click would change show it on their state layer at 50%: `--row-selected` for rows joining, `--row-read` over selected rows leaving (dashed Highlight outlines in forced colors). Hover is kept by the list as well as by `:hover` (`features/list/rowHover.ts`): when a row leaves under a pointer that hasn't moved, the row that slides in takes `data-hover` and its hover actions at once, so clicking again acts on it. A leaving row's controls take no pointer events, and focus inside it moves to the row taking its place. A section header ("Today", "Pinned") whose rows are all leaving folds away with them on the same DefaultSpatial grid collapse, so nothing below steps when the rows leave the cache. The selection toolbar sits over the list app bar in one grid cell (`.list-head`), so selecting or clearing never moves the list. Read and unread ease between weights (Google Sans Flex is variable). Connected button groups turn an option's icon into the check, or grow the check in from the start, so the segment never jumps; RSVP answers show the moment they're picked and go back only if sending fails.
- **Refresh** turns its arrow while the list reloads and finishes the turn on DefaultSpatial, so even an instant refresh reads as done. A theme change cross-fades the whole app on SlowEffects (a view transition), like Android.
- Reduced motion keeps the cross-fades (150ms) and drops turns, rolls, pops and width changes.
- Loading indicator morph, wavy progress and the empty-state shapes keep their spring timings.

## Performance

Motion has to hold up at 120 and 144Hz, and at 60 in Safari, which caps pages there.

- Move things with `transform` and `opacity`. Layout properties (width, height, grid tracks, margins) only animate on small, bounded elements: a button, a chip, one row, the rail's More list. Never the list or the reading pane.
- JS motion is time based (`requestAnimationFrame` with elapsed time) or Web Animations. Nothing steps on `setTimeout` or counts frames.
- Spring curves in `tokens.css` are `linear()` with points placed where the curve bends. Regenerate them from the spring if a damping ratio changes; don't hand-trim points.
- List rows are memoized. They skip `content-visibility: auto` on purpose: rows vary from 72 to 200px, so a placeholder height made the list jump as rows scrolled in. Give them props that only change when the row does: unread counts change on every read, so rows get the label list keyed by name and colour, not the raw query data.
- Build `Intl` formatters and HCT colours once (module level or cached). A row renders often and a formatter per call costs more than the row.
- Gestures write styles to the DOM directly while the finger moves (pull to refresh, swipe) and only set React state when something commits.
- In WebKit, a style change on `<html>` restyles the whole app (about 25ms), and a custom property on `.app` that the app reads does too. Write there only when the value changes, as the rail width does.
- The first frame of the shell has its fonts: every face is `font-display: block`, so the boot screen stays up until the text and symbols fonts are in (`theme/fonts.ts`, at most 2s). Nothing on the shell's first frame transitions in, including the first labels' counts and unread weight.
- First runs should feel like tenth runs. `warmup.tsx` runs once the inbox is up, in idle slices: motion tokens, a Base UI menu, dialog and snackbar opened off the document so their code is compiled, shape outlines and the Inbox morphs, the seed swatches' colours, the latin-ext font. Read motion tokens through `ui/motion.ts` (cached; a fresh read forces a document style recalc) and shape paths through `shapePath()`.
- The frame that answers a click or key stays small; heavy rendering follows in React's sliced renders (`useDeferredValue`). Opening a thread paints the row and pane first and the pane's contents just after (`MainArea`, then the messages in `ThreadPane`); a change to the list on screen or to the open thread (a row leaving, a read, a star) re-renders rows and messages after the frame that showed it; a different list or thread still shows at once. Memoized parts (rows, the message area) only re-render when their own data changes: list pages share thread objects by id (`shareListById`), and email bodies and banners key their parsing on the message's content, not the object a label change replaces.
- A theme change renders under its cross-fade (`theme/crossfade.ts`): the old frame is captured, then React re-renders and the new colours apply while it is still up, with transitions off so hundreds of surfaces don't each animate under the fade. Open emails restyle their frame in place instead of reloading.
- A row that leaves keeps its slot until its collapse has played, whatever else prunes the list meanwhile (`pruneAfterCollapse`).
- A category's badge and header count its unread mail in the inbox (`useCategoryUnread` in `features/list/CategoryTabs.tsx`), the mail its tab lists. Gmail's category labels also count archived mail, which can run to thousands over an empty tab.
- Counts never step backwards because the server is behind: unread changes made here hold over fresh labels until the server's count moves (`withPendingUnread` in `data/client.ts`). The rail grows for a longer count but only narrows when its names or layout change.

## Components

`src/app/ui/`: Button (filled, tonal, outlined, text, elevated; XS/S/M; round/square), IconButton (standard/filled/tonal/outlined, narrow/default/wide, toggle), Fab (M3 Expressive extended FAB sizes from ExtendedFab*Tokens: S 56/16px corners/TitleMedium, M 80/20px/TitleLarge, L 96/28px/HeadlineSmall; corners square off a step on press. Compose is S everywhere, with a solid pencil; in the rail it sits flat and lifts on hover), ConnectedButtonGroup (surface container, selected primary as a full pill; pressing moves only the inner corners and widens the segment 15%), split Send button (SplitButtonSmallTokens: inner 4 rounding to 12 on hover and press, trailing turns circular when open), Chip (filter chips morph 12 resting, 8 pressed, full when selected; input chips 32/8 LabelLarge), Menu (M3 Expressive: no outer container, each section its own surface-container-low level-2 surface 2px apart, 16 outside / 8 between, the unhovered sections tighten to 8; items LabelLarge 44 min with 12/4 corners; selection is tertiary container with a leading check, never checkboxes; a MenuSeparator starts a new section), Select, Dialog, side sheet, Tooltip, Snackbar (bottom start, 16px corners), Checkbox, Switch, Radio, Tabs, TextField, Avatar, LoadingIndicator, LinearProgress, EmptyArt (MaterialShapes with an icon). Toolbars group actions with 12px of space, never divider rules. Tooltips and menus never draw keyboard shortcuts (Gmail doesn't); buttons and menu items carry them as `aria-keyshortcuts` instead. The exception is compose, which says "Bold (⌘B)", "Send (⌘Enter)" and "Add Cc recipients (⌘⇧C)" in the tooltip text, as Gmail does. Shortcuts are listed in the `?` dialog and the command palette. In the `?` dialog each single-key shortcut's keycap is a button: it turns secondary-container and reads "Press a key" while it listens, a changed key gets a trailing reset, and every hint (palette, `aria-keyshortcuts`) shows the person's keys through `currentShortcut()`. Badges are M3 large badges: 16px, `error`, at most four characters ("999+"), anchored at the icon's center and kept inside the 80px rail.

## Layout

The rail runs the full height and owns its header: menu button and the brand mark on the top row (level with search), Compose below. The top app bar spans only the content: the 56px search pill (see Search and toolbars), then help, settings and account; the mark moves into it only while the rail is collapsed. The list has a one-row app bar (title and unread line leading; filter, refresh and More trailing, with Select in More) that tints when the list scrolls. Top bar 64, M3 Expressive wide navigation rail (WideNavigationRail.kt): as wide as its widest item (220-360, rounded up to the 4px grid) with 56px items 20px in and an active indicator that hugs icon and label (16 + 24 + 8 + label + 16, full round, grows in from the start), counts outside the pill; 96 collapsed with 64px items 4px apart, a 56x32 indicator and LabelMedium labels; 40px below the header; menu button, FAB and every icon share a 48px centre line; only the menu button expands or collapses it, and the collapsed rail's More opens the expanded rail as a modal over the content; modal under 1024px with the menu button stacked over the FAB, list and reading pane with a draggable divider (list width scales 400-600px by default). Under 600px it becomes Gmail Android's M3 Expressive layout: menu and avatar outside a 56px search field (no search-options button), bundles, a list title bar that scrolls away under the search bar while the search bar leaves on scroll down and returns on scroll up (transforms only, `features/shell/phoneChrome.ts`), Compose FAB that shrinks on scroll, pinned Reply/Forward bar with 56px medium buttons. An open conversation hides the search bar: its toolbar (back, then archive, delete, mark unread and More at the trailing edge) is the only app bar, and Snooze, Reply later and Newer/Older move into More. Wide lists switch to Gmail web's one-line rows by container query.

**Interface size** (80 to 125%, `shared/uiScale.ts`) is CSS `zoom` on the app's iframe, written by the host page only when it changes. The frame takes it like browser zoom (Cmd + and Cmd -): it keeps filling the window and the app lays out in the room the zoom leaves, so nothing inside knows about it; zoom inside the app's own document would split vh, popover anchoring and every getBoundingClientRect from CSS lengths. WebKit shrinks a zoomed frame's box with the zoom, so the host measures the box after zooming and grows it back by the same factor where that happened. The app checks the result by its viewport width (its width times the zoom equals the frame's width on screen); an engine that fails that goes back to 100% and drops the setting; phones stay at 100%. Settings shows it as a connected button group; Quick settings, too narrow for five labels, as a stepped slider that applies on release, since a zoom mid-drag would resize the slider under the pointer. In a text-only button group only the selected option makes room for its check.

**Spacing.** Everything sits on a 4px grid (2px only for segmented gaps and hairlines). One gutter, `--app-gutter` in `tokens.css` (16, 8 under 600px), separates the window edge, the panes and the search bar: the reading pane sits a gutter right of the list (the drag handle is the gutter), a gutter under the search pill and a gutter in from the window's end and bottom; pages (Storage, Subscriptions, Attachments, Purchases, Follow-ups) start on the same line as the pane. Quick settings, snackbars and the offline banner float a gutter from the window. Alignment lines, measured, not eyeballed:
- **Rows:** 16 in, a 40px lead column, text at 72 from the row's edge. The list title, the Reply later card, page headers and section headers ("Today", "Large attachments") follow it: marks centre on the avatar column, titles start at 72, section headers and filter chips at 16.
- **Thread:** the subject, every sender name (open or collapsed: a collapsed 32px photo centres on the 40px column), bodies, attachments and the reply buttons start on one line; the star ends where the messages' More buttons do; the toolbar's first and last glyphs sit on the message cards' edges.
- **Search and palette:** results share the field's three lines: leading glyph centred 28 in, text at 56, trailing glyph or date ending 16 from the end.
- **Menus** (SegmentedMenuTokens): 12 between icon and label; group labels, titles and search fields in a menu share the items' icon column. Account rows lead with a 44px column so every row's text starts together.
- **Dialogs and sheets:** a close button's glyph ends on the content's trailing edge. Compose's title, fields, body and Send share a 20px edge.
- **Nesting:** an inner surface's radius is the outer radius minus its inset (the settings pane 16 inside the 28 dialog, attachment thumbnails 12 inside 16 cards).
- Chips follow AssistChipTokens (8 leading, 8 between, 16 trailing); spaces between chips are 8.

## Reading pane

- **At a glance** (empty pane, `features/glance/Glance.tsx`): a 88px time-of-day mark (Sunny, Cookie 9, Cookie 12 or Clover 8 in tertiary container), the date in LabelLarge tertiary, a greeting in Google Sans Flex 500 with ROND 100 (clamped 32 to 45px), then "Worth a look" is a segmented list of 72px rows, each led by a 40px MaterialShape in its role colour (codes primary, deliveries and bills tertiary, invites secondary, questions primary); codes carry a Copy chip over the trailing edge. "Follow-ups due" lists reminders that came due, on error container. Sent-mail nudges stay in the list (and the Follow-ups page), not here. The sentence under the greeting only appears when there are no rows ("Nothing needs you right now"), so it never restates them. Each kind has its own freshness rule in `smart/freshness.ts`, read from dates in the email (a delivery estimate, a due date, the event's day) as well as when it arrived: a code lives 20 minutes (less if the email says so); a delivered package shows only the day it lands; out for delivery lasts into the next day; a shipment drops three days past its estimate, or after 10 quiet days without one, and anything about a package older than 30 days is history; paid and autopay bills drop (autopay after two days), others stay until a week past due; invites need to be unread and for an event still ahead. Rows rank by what needs you soonest (codes, delivery problems, arriving today, bills due within two days, events today or tomorrow, late packages, then the rest), at most six. Packages come from `smart/packages.ts`, the same reader as Purchases, at their newest known status (see Package card): a "Shipped" email whose package has since been delivered shows Delivered, or nothing. Selecting rows swaps the board for the selection summary. The mark holds a picture when there is one (`wallpaper/Hero.tsx`): your own picture if you set one, else your profile photo (the account in view, as the top-right avatar shows it; in All inboxes or a circle, the account this tab signed in with), else the time-of-day icon as above, which is also what a letter placeholder photo gets. The layout never changes: the mark grows from 88 to 112px (112 read as the same element grown; 120 and 128 started to push the greeting down) and becomes a Cookie 9 on a 6px ring of tertiary container with the photo inside, the date and greeting below it on the same left edge. Cookie 9 won over Sunny, Clover 8 and a circle: its soft scallops read as a frame without cutting into a face. Your own picture is cropped to keep its subject in its setting, eyes about two fifths down (`cropFor`); a profile photo is placed the way the avatar places it. When a picture appears or changes, the grow and the shape morph run together on the 0.8-damped spring (520ms); an account switch keeps the old photo until the new one is known, then morphs. A page load shows it already grown: while the profile photo is checked, a surface-container-highest placeholder of the final shape and size holds its place. Reduced motion fades instead. It is a control ("Your profile photo, options" or "Your picture, options", with a tooltip): click, Enter, right click or a long press opens a menu with Change picture, Use colors from this picture (Shuffle colors from this picture once they're in use), and on your own picture Use your profile photo (Remove picture when there's no profile photo), which deletes it from this browser. The ring turns tertiary while the menu is open or it has focus. A picture file held over it thickens the ring to 10px in primary and scales it to 1.06; dropping it sets your picture. The time-of-day mark, when shown, is the same control with Add a picture.
- **Package card** (`smart/SmartCards.tsx`, `smart/tracking.ts`, `smart/trackingStore.ts`): emails that share a tracking number (or, without one, an order number) are one package. The big status is the newest across all of them; a package never goes backwards (a retailer's late "Shipped" can't undo "Delivered"), except through a problem. When that news came from another email, a line under it says when and from whom ("Today at 2:37 PM, from Swiftlane"). Under the tracker, Updates is a segmented list of each step once, newest first, at the first email that said it: a 10px dot (12px with a 4px halo in primary for the current step, tertiary when delivered, error for a problem), the step in TitleSmall and "when · who" in BodySmall. The row for the open email ends in "This email"; the others are buttons with a chevron that open their email, with list-row hover, press and focus states. List rows and At a glance read the same merged status.
  The card's ⋮ ("Package options", also on each Purchases tile) sets the status by hand: Mark as delivered first, the five statuses under Change status, Use status from emails to go back, each with Undo. A hand-set status beats every email before it and gives way only to a newer email that moves the package on; the line under the status says "set by you" and it takes its own step in Updates. "Add to Packages" in a message's More menu and a row's context menu files the email as a package in one step, with Undo, using what its emails say (store, order number, tracking number, carrier): by tracking number, else order number, else the email itself. It then gets the card, linking, Purchases and At a glance like any other. On an email added that way both menus offer Remove from Packages instead.
  Records live in extension storage (`bg.tracking.v1`, newest 150, 90 days). They fill from mail already loaded first: every conversation and list that passes through the query cache, and the notification check that runs on new mail, so a "Delivered" arriving in the inbox updates its package without a search. Only then a background search (`data/backgroundQueue.ts`) for the exact number, quoted, spaced and compact; when no result's subject says a status, the newest one is read in full. Delivered is final and never searched again; otherwise a search holds 20 minutes out for delivery, 2 hours after a problem, 4 hours shipped, 8 hours ordered. Nothing older than 30 days is searched, and searches stop at 8 an hour across tabs and 12 per tab session.
- **Reply bar**: Reply, Reply all and Forward are a standard M3 Expressive button group of medium buttons (56, full round, 12 on press) pinned to the bottom of the pane over a 24px fade into the pane colour. With a mouse, the pressed button widens about 15% and its neighbours give the room back (padding on FastSpatial), as in ButtonGroup.kt. Suggested replies trail them and wrap above when the pane narrows. On phones it is the surface-container sheet with the buttons sharing the width.
- **Subject**: HeadlineMedium emphasized (28/36, weight 500, ROND 100), HeadlineLarge (32/40) once the pane is 840 wide; the pane is a `pane` size container. Labels follow it as input chips. On panes 960 and wider, once the subject scrolls out of view it fades into the middle of the thread toolbar in TitleMedium (an IntersectionObserver, opacity and an 8px rise, laid out all the time so the actions never move).
- **Message header**: 40px photo, then the sender in TitleMedium on one line that never wraps: the address follows it in BodySmall and stays hidden until you point at the header or tab into it (always shown on touch screens), and it gives way before the name truncates. Below it, "to ..." opens the details popover. The date, Unsubscribe, Reply and More trail it. Under 700 wide the date becomes relative ("4 hours ago") and Unsubscribe moves to More, where it is also listed on wider panes.
- **Message stack**: a segmented list of cards (2px apart, 16 outer and 4 inner corners). Collapsed messages are 56px rows on a 44/160/1fr/16/auto grid. ## Compose

`features/compose/`, styles in `compose.css`. One form (`ComposeForm.tsx`) runs the docked window, full screen and the inline reply.

- **Docked windows**: 560 wide, 640 tall at most, a bottom sheet (28 on top, square at the screen edge) on surface-container-lowest (container-high in dark), level 3. They rise 24px into place on DefaultSpatial with a FastEffects fade and drop away on DefaultEffects when closed or sent. The dock runs from 16px off each edge and keeps every window on screen: when the open ones don't fit, the one used longest ago folds to its bar (`useFit`), and restoring a bar folds another if it has to. Windows beside one that opens, folds or leaves slide to their new place with a translate (DefaultSpatial), never a layout animation.
- **Minimize** folds the window into its title bar: a clip from the full sheet to the 280x56 bar at its bottom trailing corner on FastSpatial, the content fading out on FastEffects and the surface easing to the bar's surface-container-high. Only when the fold lands does the layout swap to the bar, in the same frame, and the bar's title fades in. Restore runs it backwards on DefaultSpatial with the content fading in after a 20% hold. The bar has Restore (keyboard_arrow_up) and Close; clicking anywhere on it restores. Focus stays on the window's Restore button.
- **Header**: the title (TitleMedium, the subject once there is one), then autosave in LabelMedium: "Saving…" only when a save takes longer than 600ms, otherwise it goes straight to "Saved", and the three states share one grid cell and cross-fade, so the title never shifts. A failed save says "Couldn't save" in error with Retry.
- **Expanded composer** (full screen, `ComposeRail.tsx`): a page, the whole window less a 16px gutter all round at every width. From 880px wide it is a grid with even spacing: the writing column fills the space left of the side rail (24% of the page, 360 to 440; 320 under 1200), 40 from the page's edge, top and foot and from the rail (32 under 1200) at every width; the rail sits 12 from the page on top, end and bottom, with Saved, Exit full screen and Close in its top row, level with its first heading. The subject is the page title, first in reading and Tab order: Google Sans Flex 500 32/40 ROND 100, a textarea that wraps (Enter moves to the message). From, To, Cc and Bcc sit under it in one tonal strip (surface-container-low, 16 corners) with the recipient chips and their photos, both the column's full width. The text keeps a 720px measure (about 80 characters) from the column's start. Formatting is docked open above the text and sticks to the top while the page scrolls; the send row (Send, Help me write, attach, link, emoji, signature, templates, Discard) stays at the foot of the column, as in the docked window; send later and Send with reminder are in Send's menu. The rail is its own surface (16 corners) and slides in 24px on DefaultSpatial after an 80ms hold. It holds only what helps while writing: the mail you're answering or forwarding as a readable card (quote it into the message, below the signature); Recent with the first person you're writing to (their latest four conversations, the one you're answering left out, as segmented rows with subject, date and two lines of snippet; opening one docks the draft and shows the conversation; pulsing rows while they load, a plain line when there are none or they can't load); attachments as tiles two across (88px previews, picture thumbnails, an Add files tile, drop anywhere); and a word count with reading time at the foot. Expanding and shrinking never scale anything: growing, the page is laid out at full size and a clip grows from the docked window's box on DefaultSpatial while a still copy of the window fades off it (FastEffects) and the page's content fades in; Exit full screen, Escape and opening a Recent row lay the docked window out in place and fold a still copy of the page (with its scrim) down onto it, its content veiled on FastEffects, then fade the copy to show the window. The fold and that fade are one effect, keyframes sampled from the DefaultSpatial spring (`easingFn`): Safari runs a clip on the main thread and a fade off it, so as two animations a busy frame let the fade run ahead of the fold. The copy sits in a holder covering both boxes, since on a wide window the docked window reaches past the page. An idle warm-up builds an invisible window and page once, so the first expand costs what later ones do. Narrower windows keep the single centred column, and phones keep the plain full-screen form.
- **Fields**: From (a select when there's a choice, named in merged inboxes), To, Cc and Bcc, Subject; BodyLarge, outline rules under each. An inline reply folds its recipients into one line ("To Hannah Kowalczyk, cc Yuki Tanabe") until clicked, and opens them whenever they're empty or the inbox is merged (the sending account always shows there).
- **Writing**: BodyLarge 16/24, primary caret, quotes as a 12-corner primary 6% tint (the mail keeps Gmail's own quote styling). A click in the empty space under the text writes at the end of what you wrote, never inside the signature; reopening a draft puts the caret there too. Paste keeps meaning and drops looks (`paste.ts`): bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, links, lists and headings stay; fonts, sizes, colours and Docs' wrappers go; pictures that only exist on the copier's machine are dropped. ⌘⇧V pastes plain text, and a pasted web address over selected text makes it a link.
- **Formatting toolbar** (`FormatToolbar.tsx`): an M3 Expressive floating toolbar (standard: surface-container, full round, level 3) over the bottom of the body. It rises out of its button (translate 12 and 0.9 scale to rest on DefaultSpatial) and folds back on FastSpatial; the body gains 72px of bottom room while it's open. Toggle tools on show it: secondary container with corners squared from 20 to 12. The text colour button shows the current colour on its bar. One Tab stop, arrow keys inside. - **Shortcuts**: ⌘Enter sends, ⌘⇧Enter opens schedule send, ⌘⇧C and ⌘⇧B open Cc and Bcc focused, ⌘K inserts or edits a link, ⌘B/I/U, ⌘⇧7 and ⌘⇧8 lists, ⌘⇧9 quote (again to remove), ⌘[ and ⌘] indent, ⌘⇧X strikethrough, ⌘\\ clears formatting, Tab and ⇧Tab nest list items. - **Link dialog** (`LinkDialog.tsx`): text and address, Enter applies; editing a link adds Remove link. Addresses without a scheme get https://, an email address gets mailto:.

- **Signatures** (`signatures.ts`, `signatureDom.ts`, `SignatureSettings.tsx`): any number of named signatures, and for each send-as address one default for new mail and one for replies and forwards, like Gmail.
